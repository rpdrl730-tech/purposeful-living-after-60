Gear & tools — what to look for

No hype product pushes. Clear criteria for mats, bands, dumbbells, freelance platforms, and design/selling tools — and free ways to start today.

Browse articles

Use this page when an article points you to equipment or platforms. We are not tied to one store. These are plain-English buying criteria so you can choose well — and free alternatives when you do not want to buy yet.

We may add affiliate links here later. Today this guide is unpaid advice. Always check current product reviews, return policies, and platform fees yourself.

Exercise mat

What to look for

  • Thickness: about ½ inch (10–15 mm) for knee comfort
  • Grip: non-slip on bare floors; not too slick when you sweat
  • Size: long enough for your height when lying down
  • Clean: wipeable surface
  • Storage: rolls or folds without fighting you

Free / cheap start: a folded blanket or large towel on a non-slip rug. Move to a mat when balance work needs a clearer surface.

Light dumbbells or resistance bands

Dumbbells

  • Start very light (often 2–5 lb / 1–2 kg per hand)
  • Comfortable grip; not slippery
  • Adjustable sets save space if you like progress in small jumps

Resistance bands

  • A set with several strengths (light / medium / heavy)
  • Fabric or quality latex; check handles and door anchor reviews
  • Inspect for cracks; replace if damaged

Free start: filled water bottles or soup cans for rows and presses.

Freelance & course platforms

Choosing a marketplace or course host

  • Large, active buyers in your skill area
  • Clear fee schedule (percentage or monthly) — no mystery “activation” fees
  • Secure payments paid out to you after work is done
  • Good help docs / human support
  • You can delete your account and export your content

Examples people often start with (research current terms yourself): freelance boards for services; tutoring platforms for teaching; established course hosts if you sell lessons. Pick one, learn it well, ignore the rest at first.

Design & selling tools for digital products

What beginners actually need

  • A word processor you already know (export to PDF)
  • Optional: a simple design tool with templates for a nicer cover
  • A selling platform that hosts the file, takes payment, and delivers the download
  • Gentle learning curve and tutorials for beginners
  • Transparent fees and reliable payouts

Free path: write in Google Docs or Word → File → Download PDF → sell via a reputable digital-product marketplace that gives you a share link. Add fancy design later.
