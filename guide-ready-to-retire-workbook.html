The Ready-to-Retire Checklist & Workbook

Five sections — money, Mondays, connection, health, and meaning — turned into simple worksheets so you can walk into retirement with a plan on paper, not just in your head.

Back to the article

This workbook turns the five questions from our article 5 Questions to Answer Before You Retire into fill-in pages. Work through them with a pen, a partner if you have one, and no rush. The goal is clarity on paper — not perfection.

This workbook is general guidance, not financial, tax, legal, or medical advice. Before making irreversible decisions about pensions, benefits, investments, or healthcare coverage, speak with a qualified professional who knows your situation.

How to use this workbook

  1. Print it or copy the tables into a notebook.
  2. Complete Sections 1–5 in order if you can; money first often lowers anxiety for the rest.
  3. Mark one action at the end of each section you can finish in the next 14 days.
  4. Revisit every 6–12 months, or whenever your plans change.

Section 1 · Money: Do I have enough — and for how long?

List expected yearly spending and yearly income. Round numbers are fine; honesty beats precision.

1A · Annual spending estimate

Write a yearly amount (or monthly × 12) for each category.

  • Housing (mortgage/rent, tax, insurance, HOA): $ _______________ / year
  • Utilities & communications: $ _______________ / year
  • Food & household: $ _______________ / year
  • Transport: $ _______________ / year
  • Healthcare (premiums, out-of-pocket, dental, vision, meds): $ _______________ / year
  • Insurance (life, long-term care, home, auto): $ _______________ / year
  • Travel & hobbies: $ _______________ / year
  • Gifts, family support, charity: $ _______________ / year
  • Debt payments: $ _______________ / year
  • Other / buffer (10–15% is wise): $ _______________ / year
  • Total estimated spending: $ _______________ / year

1B · Annual income in retirement

  • Social Security / state pension (estimate at planned claim age): $ _______________ / year
  • Workplace pensions / annuities: $ _______________ / year
  • Part-time or online work: $ _______________ / year
  • Rental or other passive income: $ _______________ / year
  • Planned draw from savings/investments: $ _______________ / year
  • Total estimated income: $ _______________ / year

1C · The gap check

  • Income − spending = $ _______________ (surplus or shortfall)
  • Years I want this plan to cover (e.g. to age 90+): _______________
  • Have I allowed for inflation? ☐ Yes ☐ Not yet
  • Have I listed large one-off costs (car, roof, move, care)? ☐ Yes ☐ Not yet

If there is a shortfall, which levers will I explore? (work longer, delay benefits, downsize, cut large costs, adjust draw rate, side income)

Master checklist · Ready to retire?

  • ☐ Spending and income lists drafted
  • ☐ Gap (if any) has at least one planned lever
  • ☐ Inflation and longevity considered
  • ☐ Ordinary-day plan written
  • ☐ At least one social/community anchor in place or scheduled
  • ☐ Healthcare coverage path understood
  • ☐ Movement habit started (even 10 minutes)
  • ☐ Meaning / direction statement written
  • ☐ Partner/family conversation held (if applicable)
  • ☐ Professional advice booked where money decisions are irreversible
